01The Idea

The name is the whole strategy.

We do not need to invent this. The salon already says it, in their own words.

Beautiful style is achieved through mastery of contrasting elements: dark and light, wavy and straight, dynamic and still.

Duality Salon

That line is the brand strategy, and it belongs somewhere prominent on the new site. Everything in this document is that idea applied. It is true of the salon three times over: Sue and Leah are mother and daughter, the room is soft and industrial at once, and the work is both technical precision and personal care.

Soft

Warm, personal, unhurried. The half that makes someone feel looked after.

  • Sand and ivory grounds, brass warmth
  • Cormorant Garamond, light and high contrast
  • Natural window light, skin tones, texture
  • Language about conversation and care

Industrial

Clean, precise, confident. The half that says these people know exactly what they are doing.

  • Ink grounds and hairline rules
  • Jost, geometric and even in colour
  • Straight edges, generous space, no clutter
  • Language about precision and craft

Use both on every surface. All soft goes generic salon. All industrial goes cold. The tension between them is the brand.

02Positioning

Sue and Leah, front and centre.

The client's own direction, and the right one. Most salon sites lead with a stock photo of hair. This one leads with two named people and a family story, which is the one thing competitors cannot copy.

Sue is Artistic Director, with four decades behind the chair and a background as a L'ANZA educator training stylists up and down the East Coast. Leah is the owner. She grew up in it and now leads the education side. That hand-off between generations is the story both the homepage and the About page should carry.

Three proof points the build already uses well, all worth protecting: 100+ years of combined experience across five stylists, established 2020 in Williamsville, and the promise that the salon never overbooks. That last one is the strongest thing on the page, because it is a specific operational commitment rather than an adjective.

Lead with

  • Named stylists with real faces
  • The mother and daughter thread
  • Never overbooking, stated plainly
  • Education, and the L'ANZA roots
  • Long-standing client relationships

Avoid

  • Stock salon photography of any kind
  • Luxury filler such as pamper or indulge
  • Leading with price or promotions
  • Anonymous "our team of experts"
  • Exclamation marks anywhere
03Colour

Warm ground, ink structure, one brass accent.

These are the tokens already in the build. Sand and ivory carry the soft half and should cover most of any page. Ink carries the industrial half and does the structural work. Brass is the only accent, and it is used sparingly.

Worth noting: the sand ground is almost exactly the salon's own wall colour, and the brass matches the woven pendant lights. The palette was read from the room, not chosen for it.

04Typography

One serif with warmth, one sans with discipline.

The pairing carries the duality without anyone having to explain it. Cormorant Garamond is elegant, high contrast and soft. Jost is geometric, even and clean. Headlines in one, everything functional in the other, and never the reverse.

Display · Cormorant Garamond · 300 / 400 · Roman and Italic
Colour, style and self-care in Williamsville

Headlines, stylist names, pull quotes. Cormorant is light and high contrast, so it needs size and air to work. Never below 20px, and the italic carries one emphasised word rather than a whole line.

Interface · Jost · 200 to 500
Book an appointment · Curly and textured hair · Hand-tied extensions · Balayage and dimensional colour · Wednesday to Saturday

Navigation, buttons, labels, body copy, forms, captions. Jost is even in colour, which is what keeps the pages feeling clean next to the serif. Uppercase labels take 0.28em tracking. Body copy never drops below 16px, and weight 300 is for large text only.

05Layout

The page alternates, the way the brand does.

The single most important structural rule in the build is the band rhythm. Cream section, then ink section, then cream again, the whole way down. That is not decoration. It is the soft and industrial pairing made structural, so the page performs the brand idea rather than describing it.

Two rules keep it working. Never place two ink bands next to each other, and give every band enough vertical padding that the alternation reads as deliberate rather than stripey. Dark bands carry the moments that need weight: pricing, the never-overbook promise, education, and the closing call to action.

Recurring devices

The brass sealGraphic device
The circular monogram works as a stamp on dark bands, set large and low in opacity behind content, or small and solid as a badge. Use it once per band at most. It is the only ornament the brand needs.
Italic emphasisHeadline pattern
Every section headline italicises one phrase in Cormorant: a thoughtful conversation, the Duality team, have to say. One phrase per headline, never two, and never a whole line. It is what makes the headings feel written rather than assembled.
Eyebrow labelSection marker
A small-caps Jost label sits above every section heading, tracked wide in stone or brass. It gives the long page structure without adding rules everywhere.
The offset cardImage treatment
A cream card overlapping the edge of a photograph, as used on the mother and daughter block. It softens a hard photo edge and is the one place the layout breaks its own grid. Keep it rare, two or three times across a page.
The price ledgerPricing pattern
Service name left, price right, hairline between rows. No boxes, no badges, no strikethroughs. Honest pricing presented plainly is the point, and it is reinforced by the line about the stylist confirming the final price at consultation.
The step rowProcess pattern
Four steps across, separated by hairlines rather than arrows or numbers in circles. Book, consultation, service, at-home care. Numbers stay small and in brass.
Full-bleed photo stripGallery pattern
Four images edge to edge with no gaps, running the full width. It gives the page a breath between reading sections and shows the room without a formal gallery.

Buttons

  • Primary: solid ink, ivory text, no radius
  • Secondary: hairline outline, brass text
  • On ink bands: hairline outline, ivory text
  • Labels in Jost, tracked, sentence case

Spacing

  • Band padding scales with viewport, never fixed
  • One hairline weight across the whole site
  • Gaps of 2px between colour blocks, not 20px
  • Let whitespace separate, not borders
06Photography

Real room, real people, real light.

The room does the brand's work already. Warm cream walls and woven drum pendants set against a blacked-out ceiling and exposed ductwork, with live plants softening every corner and warm wood underfoot. That is the soft-and-industrial idea, built. Shoot it as it is, and keep the ceiling in frame on wider shots, because it is the industrial half of the palette.

The team wears all black in every photo, which reads as deliberate and should stay that way. It keeps group shots clean and lets the warm room and the hair carry the colour.

Of everything supplied, the education-class photo is the most valuable. Every stylist's bio claims ongoing training, and that one image proves it rather than stating it.

Do

  • Shoot in the salon, in its own light
  • Portraits at eye level, relaxed, to camera
  • Hands working, tools in frame
  • Leave space for type to sit
  • Keep skin tones warm and true

Don't

  • Heavy filters or cool blue grading
  • Stock imagery, even as placeholder
  • Tight crops with no breathing room
  • Busy backgrounds fighting the subject
  • Mixed portrait styles on one page

One rule for the team page: every portrait shot the same way, same distance, same background treatment. Mismatched portraits are the fastest way to make a good team look unserious.

07Voice

Expert, warm, and never salesy.

The site already has the right instinct with lines like "every great style begins with a thoughtful conversation." Keep it. Write the way Sue or Leah would talk to someone in the chair: confident about the craft, genuinely interested in the person.

Sounds like Duality

  • "Every great style begins with a thoughtful conversation."
  • "We never overbook. Ever."
  • "We'll tell you what will actually work with your hair."
  • "Curly hair is its own craft, and we've trained for it."

Doesn't

  • "Indulge in the ultimate pampering experience."
  • "Transform your look today!"
  • "Book now and save 20%."
  • "Our team of beauty professionals."
08The Team

Bios for the new About page.

Tightened from the copy the salon supplied, kept in their own facts and their own warmth. Sue and Leah lead, as requested. Each one closes on something personal, which is what makes a team page worth reading.

Artistic Director · Mother

Sue Dicky

40 years behind the chair

Sue has spent four decades behind the chair and most of that time teaching other people how to do it well. She travelled the East Coast as a L'ANZA educator, training and inspiring stylists along the way, and she still hosts classes here at the salon.

What she values most is the clients she has known for years. Outside the salon you will find her sewing a new project or volunteering with her church's children's program.

Owner & Stylist · Daughter

Leah Blachaniec

Behind the chair since 2013

Leah is the daughter of a lifelong stylist, so you could fairly say this is in her DNA. She grew up surrounded by the work and turned that into salon ownership, and she has spent six years building the team here.

She leads on continued education, which is how the whole salon keeps getting better. Away from work she is caring for her houseplants, tending a small summer garden, and baking.

Stylist

Amanda Pohlman

Almost 20 years in the industry

Amanda followed her dreams into hairstyling in her thirties and has been growing her craft ever since. She loves a polished blowout that shows off the colour and the cut underneath, and she has taken class after class to keep refining her technique alongside the rest of the team.

When she is not at the salon she is travelling with her husband or spending time with family.

Stylist

Darcy Ragione

Nearly 30 years in the industry

Darcy grew up watching her grandmother give perms, and that is what pulled her into hairstyling. She builds custom cuts and styles around each client rather than working from a template.

She is a regular in the classroom, sharing ideas with other stylists and bringing new techniques back to her clients, and she continues her education here at the salon. Outside work she is at the gym or with her pups.

Stylist · Curls & Vivids

Marianna Campione

Creating hair magic since 2023

Marianna is a curly girl herself, which is exactly why clients trust her with their natural texture. She helps people stop fighting their curls and start feeling confident in them, and she loves the creative side of vivid colour.

She is deep into learning every hair type and texture there is, with more classes booked. Away from the salon she travels and gets lost in a good book.
